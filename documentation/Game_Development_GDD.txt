1. GAME OVERVIEW
1.1 Concept
This is an online multiplayer hero arena game inspired by Nigerian mythology,
spiritual traditions, folklore and contemporary Nigerian environments.
Players enter a multiplayer lobby, select a playable champion, choose a spiritual
patron, customise their combat loadout and compete against other players in
team-based arena matches.
Game Development::
1
The game's identity comes from combining:
Nigerian mythology and cultural inspiration.
Distinctive playable champions.
Patron-based spiritual progression.
Fast, ability-driven combat.
Competitive team gameplay.
Nigerian-inspired urban and fantasy environments.
Character skins, cosmetics and seasonal progression.
Online social features and competitive rankings.
The central idea is that players are not controlling the gods themselves. They are
champions who receive power through a chosen spiritual patron.
1.2 Design vision
The experience should feel like a premium multiplayer game, not a small cultural
demonstration or a collection of Nigerian-themed assets.
Players should have reasons to return:
Improving their combat ability.
Discovering new champions.
Developing patron relationships.
Experimenting with charms and loadouts.
Competing with friends.
Unlocking cosmetics.
Climbing competitive rankings.
Participating in seasonal events.
1.3 Design pillars
1. Combat first

2. Nigerian identity
The characters, environments, sound design, mythology, architecture and visual

Use fictional magical abilities, stylised melee animations and supernatural effects.
Do not depend on realistic weapon simulation to make combat interesting.

5. PATRONAGE SYSTEM
This is the game's defining progression feature.
5.1 Concept
Players choose a spiritual patron from a researched catalogue of Nigerian
traditional spiritual figures and traditions.
The patron represents the source of certain enhancements available to the
champion.
A champion's combat identity and a player's spiritual patron are separate
systems.
This means the same champion can have different gameplay builds depending on
the selected patron.
5.2 Patron selection
Players must be able to:
Browse the patron catalogue.
View each patron's background.
Understand their documented cultural context.
View their gameplay identity.
Preview visual effects.
Select a patron.
Develop patron progression.
Unlock patron-associated boons and charms.
Players may change their active patron outside a match.
The game should not permanently lock players into their first choice.
5.3 Patron gameplay structure
Each patron has:
Game Development::
9
Patron Gift: A passive modification.
Patron Boons: A selection of optional enhancements. The player chooses one for
the current match.
Patron Charm Collection: A collection of unlockable combat modifiers associated
with that patron.
Patron Progression: Experience and progression milestones that unlock further
customisation.
Patrons must offer different playstyles, not simply different colours for the same
ability.
5.4 Patron progression
Use a progression system with multiple ranks.
Example:
Rank Progression
Initiate First patron gift
Devoted First additional boon
Favoured Additional charm options
Adept Alternative ability modifier
Ascendant Advanced cosmetic and visual effects
These names are temporary.
Important: progression should unlock options and sidegrades, not increasingly
powerful paid advantages.

Mobility charms
Improve a specific movement interaction.
Modify movement ability behaviour.
Offer alternative traversal effects.
Defensive charms
Improve a limited defensive interaction.
Modify defensive ability behaviour.
Provide situational protection.
Ability charms
Modify ability recovery.
Change the behaviour of selected abilities.
Provide alternative effects.
Utility charms
Improve support or objective interactions.
Modify team utility.

6.3 Balance requirements
Charms must create meaningful choices.
They must not simply make a player with more unlocks stronger than someone
who is new to the game.
Use sidegrades and trade-offs.
Example:
A charm might improve mobility ability recovery while reducing its maximum
duration.
Another might improve defensive utility while limiting an offensive effect.
6.4 Inventory and unlocking
Charms can be unlocked through:
Gameplay progression.
Patron progression.
Achievements.
Events.
Challenges.
Paid items must not grant exclusive competitive advantages.

8. GAME MODES
8.1 Primary mode: CONVERGENCE
The first competitive mode.
Two teams compete to control a spiritually significant objective.
Players must work together to gain and maintain control.
The objective may change location or behaviour during the match.
Victory is determined by objective score, not simply eliminations.
8.2 Secondary mode: TEAM CLASH
A team score mode.
Teams compete to reach a target score through successful combat participation.
This should be implemented after Convergence is stable.
8.3 Future mode: SHRINE CONTROL
Teams compete over multiple objectives.
Objectives may activate in different areas of the map.
This creates rotation and teamwork opportunities.
8.4 Battle Royale
Do not include Battle Royale in the first release.
The core game is a team-based arena brawler. A separate large-scale mode
would add substantial map, networking, balancing and matchmaking complexity.

9. ARENA DESIGN
Arenas are compact, carefully designed multiplayer environments.
They should be inspired by Nigerian cities, architecture and public spaces.
They are not meant to be accurate reproductions of entire cities.
9.1 Initial arena: THE CROSSING
Environment: Fictionalised Nigerian urban outdoor district.
The environment should draw inspiration from a Lagos street intersection, transit
corridor and nearby commercial district.
Required elements:
Main open objective plaza.
Several connected street routes.
Buildings of different heights.
Rooftop or elevated traversal routes where appropriate.
Covered passages.
Side routes.
Clear team spawn areas.
Distinctive environmental landmarks.
Visually readable boundaries.
Areas suitable for different champion roles.
The arena should be visually rich but optimised for competitive play.
9.2 Map layout requirements
The first arena must include:
Two team spawn regions.
Game Development::
20
One central objective area.
At least two alternative routes.
Multiple locations for cover.
Controlled verticality.
Clear navigation landmarks.
No unintended out-of-bounds areas.
No spawn locations exposed directly to opposing teams.
Keep navigation and combat readability more important than architectural
complexity.
9.3 Future arena concepts
The Mall: A multi-level commercial environment with an atrium and connected
walkways.
The Coast: A coastal Nigerian environment with open areas and distinctive terrain.
The Palace: A courtyard environment inspired by Nigerian historical architecture.
The Market: A dense urban commercial environment with several connected
routes.
The Crossroads: A supernatural environment where the architecture and world
geometry can change during a match.
9.4 Geographic and 3D source data
Do not extract or convert Google Maps Photorealistic 3D Tiles into standalone
game models.
Google's terms restrict extracting, tracing or deriving independent 3D geometry
from its map content.
Use permitted alternatives:
OpenStreetMap data with required attribution and applicable ODbL
obligations.
Licensed architectural and geographical data.
Game Development::
21
Original 3D modelling.
Properly licensed photogrammetry.
Original environmental concept art.
Independently created fictionalised building assets.
Maintain a source and licence record for every external asset.
Google 3D Maps may be useful for permitted map visualisation, but must not be
treated as a source from which to extract game geometry.
10. VISUAL DIRECTION
10.1 Art style
The visual direction should be stylised, high-quality 3D.
The target is a premium hero arena game, with a distinctive Nigerian identity.
Avoid generic fantasy characters with African names attached.
10.2 Character art
Champion designs should use:
Distinctive silhouettes.
Modern and traditional visual references where appropriate.
Nigerian fashion influences.
Cultural material references.
Recognisable accessories.
Strong animation identity.
Distinct ability effects.
Characters must not all share the same visual language.

13. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
13.1 Game engine
Recommended: Godot with Universal Render Pipeline.
Use the current supported Godot LTS release available in the development
environment.
The development team should verify the exact installed and supported version
before implementation.
Unity is recommended because it provides mature 3D workflows and official
multiplayer tooling for real-time games with smaller player counts.
